\head{Foreword}

\begingroup
\setlength{\parskip}{5pt}  
Last year, Cliff convinced me to be the Editor and to help out in the creation of last year's Wyrmtongue. I have not been quite so enthusiastic as he was in advertising for this anthology and would like to thank everyone who has nonetheless submitted to it. I am continuing the tradition of printing this anthology far later than it should have been, but hopefully not too late. If any errors are found, they are likely my fault.

This edition of the anthology begins with \textit{New World} by Ruth Raqeef. It goes into the far future, describing the encounter humans have with an unexpected alien civilisation on a planet called Helios. Next, we have an entry by our current chair of vice, Michael Porat-Bachmutsky, \textit{Tannin}, told from the perspective of an old mage that talks about his relationship with a venerable dragon. This is followed by \textit{Rust Wyrm}, by Katherine Chahbani, our future tech-priestess and who also did the art for this anthology's cover. The titular Rust Wyrm is the name of an old spaceship on its last legs, carrying freight across the stars. 

After this is Juairiyah Raqib's \textit{Close Encounters of the Wyrm Kind} a surreal story with some time travelling shenanigans, resulting from an unfortunate encounter of wyrm, with some definite inspiration from a certain webcomic. The last piece of prose is \textit{The Worm Will Wait} by Julien C H Cheung. This follows the perspective of Grace, one of four members of an alien expedition that one can't help but be nervous about.

At the end of this anthology, we have two pieces of poetry. \textit{Through the Center of the Earth} and \textit{Jörmungandr}, by Michael Porat-Bachmutsky and Alyssa Renata respectively. Many thanks to Alyssa, our current techpriest, for helping out with printing the night before Picocon. I won't describe these poems except to say that I very much enjoyed them and hope you do too.\\
I'd like to once again thank everyone who submitted an entry to the Wyrmtongue anthology, and I hope some of you reading will consider submitting entries to future editions.
\endgroup
